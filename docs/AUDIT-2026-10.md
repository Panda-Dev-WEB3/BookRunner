## M-1: held marks settle nothing

**Finding.** The mark service keeps signing marks while the oracle holds the underlying's price (session
closed), valuing the book at the held Friday price. A Junior holder whose notice fits inside the closed
session (devnet notice 15 min; the charter had a maximum notice, no minimum) redeemed at the pre-gap value
and the reopen gap fell on the holders who stayed. PoC: the sponsor's Junior lost 60% instead of 20%; the
early exiter avoided a 4k loss.

**Fix.**

- `BRTypes.MarkInput` gains `bool held`, signed: `MARK_TYPEHASH = keccak256("Mark(uint256 bookId,uint64
  periodEnd,uint256 navUsd,uint256 deployedValueUsd,uint64 flowNonce,bytes32 inventoryRoot,bytes32
  pnlJsonHash,bytes32 receiptsRoot,bool held)")`, mirrored in `packages/shared/src/eip712.ts` (`markTypes`)
  with a fixed struct-hash vector checked on both sides. A relayer cannot flip the flag.
- The mark service (`services/mark/src/domain/held.ts`, `markHeld`) sets `held` and **fails closed**: the mark
  is held when ANY of
  - the oracle signed the underlying's price as held (`PriceUpdate.held`: session closed / feed held; the signed
    bundle price when it is newer than the stored one, else the stored one);
  - the underlying's price state is unreadable;
  - the underlying's price is older than `config.maxPriceAge` at the valuation time (the oracle stopped
    publishing, e.g. it went down before signing Friday's held transition: the stored price still says
    held=false at the Friday print);
  - a desk Stock Token position the NAV values (qty > 0) is priced held or stale.

  A normal closed session is caught by the oracle's flag (it re-stamps the held price, so it is never stale);
  the engine pool is valued at the underlying's price; an Orderly venue valuation older than its bound is
  refused outright (`venueReportAge`), never marked. The flag is computed once per mark and both signed
  (`MarkInput.held`) and published in the MarkPnl statement (`pnl.held`, hashed into `pnlJsonHash`;
  optional, absent on older statements = false).
- `Book.applyMark` on a held mark applies the P&L (waterfall, backstop, drawdown kill, share prices) but
  settles no redemption bucket and no top-up round (no `Tranche.settleAtMark` call, so `lastSettledIndex`
  does not move). The first non-held mark settles every bucket due by then, and a due round, at its post-P&L
  prices, so the reopen gap is shared pro rata by everyone in the book when the session closed. Emits
  `MarkHeld(bookId, markId, heldSince, settled)`.
- **No starvation.** `Book.heldSince()` is the periodEnd of the first held mark of the current run (reset by
  any non-held mark). Once `periodEnd - heldSince >= BookLogic.MAX_HELD_DEFERRAL` (**30 days**, decision D-M1-1
  below) held marks settle again at the held valuation, unless governance has set a settle-at-price for the
  book (hook `BookLogic._governanceSettlePriceSet`, returns false until Task 7 implements the timelocked
  settle-at-price path; `TODO(Task 7)` at the hook). `requestRedeem`, claims and every other exit stay
  ungated (the M-1 tests cover requests during a hold).
- **Settlement batch.** The first non-held mark after a hold settles at most `Tranche.MAX_SETTLE_BUCKETS`
  (256) due buckets per tranche; any further due bucket (a hold spanning more than 256 distinct request
  buckets) settles at the next mark. That price is also post-hold, so the spill is benign; the Book invariant
  suite checks it (`invariant_heldMarksDeferSettlement`).
- **Defence in depth: minimum Junior notice.** `MarketCharter.validate` returns `BAD_NOTICE` when
  `juniorNoticeSeconds < config.markInterval()` (or, as before, above 30 days), mirrored in
  `services/charter` and `services/api` validation (when the interval is known), the web charter form and
  the site dashboard charter form (against the network's interval reported by the books, else > 0; web
  default 1 day) and `scripts/launch-devnet.ts` (notice raised to the interval). `MarketCharter.decide`
  re-checks it on approval against `config.markInterval()`, the exact value Book (and Tranche) cache at
  creation in the same transaction, so an interval raised between filing and approval cannot create a book
  with a notice below its own interval (`InvalidCharter("BAD_NOTICE")`; the committee can still reject, and
  an expired charter is refunded). A later config interval change never affects a created book: Book and
  Tranche settle on their cached interval, so its notice stays at or above its own interval for life (no guard
  on the config setter is needed). With at least one interval of notice a
  Junior request always bears at least one full mark period of P&L before its bucket settles. The auditor's
  alternative (notice >= the longest closed session plus one interval) was not taken: it would impose a
  multi-day minimum notice on every book, and held marks already remove the closed-session exit; the minimum
  is a backstop for a mark wrongly signed as not held, not the primary fix.
- **Senior** (no notice) is covered by the same deferral: a Senior request during a hold settles at the first
  non-held mark.

**Decisions.**

- **D-M1-1, deferral bound 30 days (controller ruling, 2026-10-18).** Above every scheduled closed session (a
  weekend plus a holiday is about 4 days; the 2001 closure was 6 days) and above the longest SEC single-name
  trading suspension (10 business days, about 14 calendar days), so neither settles a bucket at a held price.
  A bound is kept for liveness of exits: unbounded deferral would lock redemptions of a permanently held
  market. Past the bound held marks settle at the held valuation, unless governance has set a settle-at-price
  for the book (Task 7 provides that timelocked path; the hook is in place). Accepted residual until Task 7:
  a hold longer than 30 days (delisting, extended exchange halt) settles at the held price.
- **D-M1-2, fail closed (controller ruling).** Held whenever the valuation's prices are not known, live and
  fresh (list above). Cost: a mark valued while a price feed is unavailable defers settlement (bounded by
  D-M1-1) instead of settling at a possibly pre-gap price; the warning "the mark is held" names the trigger.
- **D-M1-3, minimum notice = one mark interval.** Defence in depth, see above.

**Residual.** `held` is set off-chain by the MARK_SIGNER (the same trust as the NAV it signs); the contract
does not cross-check it against the on-chain oracle state. An Orderly book's mark is flagged from the
underlying's oracle state like an engine book's (the venue's own off-hours marking is not a separate input).

## M-2: no valuation across a multiplier change

**Finding.** A split during a closed session was valued at the old per-share price times the new multiplier.
The oracle re-signs Friday's per-share price as held with a fresh timestamp, while the registry reads the
token's live `uiMultiplier()`. Once a pre-approved 10:1 split took effect over the weekend, each token was
valued at 10x, so a desk key could buy far over value with every mandate check passing and the slippage budget
charged about $0. A reverse split mirrors it on sells. PoC: the desk spends $10,000 and keeps $1,000 of value;
control: the same trade without the split reverts `SlippageTooHigh`. Marks taken in that window were inflated
the same way.

**Fix.**

- **Share basis of a price.** A per-share price is quoted in the share count of the moment it was observed,
  so after a split (or any multiplier change) it may only meet the multiplier of that moment. The basis of
  the stored price is its `publishedAt` when it is live, and `AttestedOracle.lastLiveAt(priceId)` when it is
  held. `lastLiveAt` is the publishedAt of the newest stored price signed NOT held, set on every store path
  (`push`, `pushMany`, `update`). A held price re-signs the last open-session print under a fresh timestamp,
  so its own `publishedAt` says nothing about its basis. `lastLiveAt` is derived from the signed `held` flag
  and the signed `publishedAt` only: no EIP-712 change.
- **Change time.** `StockTokenRegistry.multiplierChangedAt(token)`:
  - live mode: the token's `effectiveAt()` once it has passed (a staged change that is not active yet is
    ignored: the active multiplier is still the old one), or the last registry-side change
    (`multiplierSetAt`: an actual source switch, or a re-anchor beyond the band) if later. While a further
    change is staged, the token no longer reports when the ACTIVE multiplier took effect. If that multiplier
    is only within the band of the pre-approved next anchor (a split governance has not re-anchored yet), the
    time is unknown and the view returns `block.timestamp`: fail closed until the governance re-anchor (which
    is stamped) and a live print after it (review I-2; test
    `test_M2_stagedFollowUpChange_doesNotHideTheSplit`, which fails on the first-round registry). Enabling live
    mode requires `effectiveAt()` and `newUIMultiplier()` (Robinhood's `IScaledUIAmountNewUIMultiplier`); a
    token without them cannot be valued live (fail closed);
  - stored mode: the time of the last `setMultiplier` that changed the value, or of an actual source switch
    (`multiplierSetAt`). A no-op `setMultiplierSource` re-call and an in-band live re-anchor are not changes.
- **Registry guard.** `valueUsd` reverts `MultiplierChangedSincePrice(token, basis, changedAt)` when
  `changedAt > basis` (equal is allowed: the oracle reads the token at the block it observes). It reads
  `latest()` once (staleness checked locally, AttestedOracle's rule) and each token view once. With no
  valuation, desk hedges and desk-key flattens revert (`_hedgeValue` fails first) and `desk.valueUsd()` /
  `hedgeNotionalUsd()` revert. The guard covers both the PoC (Friday's held price re-stamped after the split:
  the basis is Friday's last live print) and a live session right at `effectiveAt` (a still-fresh price
  published before the change). It lifts with the first live price the oracle signs after the change (the
  reopen); a later hold is valued as usual (its basis is after the change). `valueUsdAt` stays a pure
  valuation at a caller-supplied price (off-chain callers apply the rule).
- **RISK flatten (review I-4, fix round 2 N-I2).** It used to tolerate a failed valuation with notional 0, i.e.
  no slippage bound at all. In a split window that let a reverse-split position be sold at 1/10 of its value
  through the automated kill path. It now calls `StockTokenRegistry.checkPriceBasis(token)` first, which reverts
  `MultiplierChangedSincePrice` in the window **whatever the price's age**. The age matters at the reopen with
  no post-split Chainlink round yet: the oracle drops the pre-split round, so the stored price goes stale and
  the stale tolerance would otherwise have applied. The flatten is deferred until the oracle reprices, then runs
  bounded by `riskMaxSlippageBps` at the repriced value. A stale price outside a split window is still
  tolerated as before. LP exits never depend on a flatten.
- **Mark service (review I-1, fix round 2 N-I1).** `services/mark/src/domain/split.ts` +
  `packages/shared/src/splitWindow.ts`. Each desk position carries its price basis (the signed bundle price's
  `publishedAt` when live, else the on-chain `lastLiveAt`) and its token's `multiplierChangedAt`, read at the
  snapshot block. A position in the window (`changedAt > basis`) is **revalued at the multiplier in force at
  the basis**: a split does not change a token's value. That multiplier comes from, in order:
  1. **on-chain** `StockTokenRegistry.basisMultiplier(token, basis)`. Registry-side changes (a stored
     `setMultiplier`, a source switch, a live split re-anchor) record the value before them and the previous
     change's time: that value is exact when it is the only registry change after the basis. A token-side
     change (live mode) gives the stored anchor, which is the pre-split multiplier until the stamped re-anchor
     (within the band of it);
  2. the token's `UIMultiplierUpdated(old, new, effectiveAt)` events, paged in 2,000-block ranges over
     `MARK_SPLIT_LOG_LOOKBACK_BLOCKS` (default 200,000);
  3. otherwise the valuation is **unavailable**: the position keeps the previous mark's value, pro rata to its
     quantity (`MarkStore.prevDeskPositions`), so the mark books no P&L for it. A held mark still runs the
     waterfall, backstop pull and drawdown kill on-chain (M-1), so a 0 would book a false loss, move backstop
     USDC and could kill the book;
  4. only with no previous value at all, 0 (logged as an error).

  It is never valued at the new multiplier. The mark is then:
  - **refused** (retryable, nothing committed) while the window is young (less than
    `MARK_SPLIT_REFUSE_MAX_SECONDS` since the change, default one mark interval) and the price is fresh: the
    oracle normally reprices at the open. The window starts at the change; when the change time is the I-2
    marker (unknown, `multiplierChangedAt` = block time), it starts at the basis, so the refusal always ends;
  - otherwise **committed held** (M-1: P&L only, no settlement): past the bound, or at once when the price is
    stale (the oracle is not publishing, so no reprice is coming).

  So M-1's 30-day bound, the desk flow gate and the settlements keep running for a token that never reprices
  (corporate-action halt, delisting). A dry run shows the window (`computed.splitWindow`) at the basis
  valuation. Refusals log at warn level, and one interval keeps a refused period inside the alerts'
  `mark_overdue` warning (critical needs two missed intervals): a weekend dividend update is an expected
  warning, not a page (review M-6).
- **Risk service (review I-4, fix round 2).** `deskHoldings` applies the same rule with the on-chain
  `basisMultiplier` (no log scans on the risk tick). A held token in the window is valued at it and flagged
  `splitWindow`, and the flatten planner never sells it (`split_window` skip). Only when the multiplier is
  unknown (valued at 0, flagged `splitUntrusted`), or the split reads fail (fail closed for that tick), do the
  desk-dependent breaches (the `DRAWDOWN` check and the `HEDGE_BAND` clock) wait. With a known basis multiplier
  the valuation is correct and every breach, including a real venue drawdown, runs as usual.
- **Oracle source.** The Chainlink per-token source divided a round seen before a multiplier change by that
  round's multiplier. That keeps a valid share price for in-band drift (a dividend reinvestment keeps the share
  unit) but is wrong for a split: the share unit changed, so the round's per-share price is in old shares, and
  the registry would value it at the new multiplier under a `publishedAt` after the change (the guard cannot
  see it). A change beyond the band now drops the observation until the feed's first round after the change.
  The band is the registry's governance `multiplierBandBps` (read at startup and every 10 min;
  `MULTIPLIER_SPLIT_BAND_BPS` = 500 is only the initial value, review M-3). A round not seen before the change
  was already dropped, and the DEX TWAP already drops a window that contains a change.
- **Engine (new finding N-1, below).** PoolEngine is gated by `StockTokenRegistry.splitGate`.

**Decisions.**

- **D-M2-1, registry guard + on-chain basis instead of an oracle-signed multiplier.** The stronger fix the
  auditor suggests (the oracle signs the multiplier it used; the registry rejects a mismatch) was evaluated
  and not taken:
  - it changes the EIP-712 `Price` type (`PRICE_TYPEHASH` bump), so every signed bundle and relayer changes
    with it: the oracle service, the mark, risk, agent and api price paths, the Redis price messages, the DB
    price rows and the demos. AttestedOracle is redeployed either way;
  - it does not fit the data model: one price id can back several registered tokens with different
    multipliers (the registry allows it), and per-share sources (Pyth, index levels, synthetic) have no token
    multiplier to sign;
  - it adds no trust boundary: the oracle service chooses the multiplier it signs exactly as it chooses the
    price, so it only protects against the service publishing a pre-change basis after the change. The split
    case of that is closed in the service (the Chainlink rule above); the in-band case is D-M2-4. The registry
    guard needs only data the contracts already trust (the signed `held` flag and `publishedAt`, the token's own
    `effectiveAt()`), and it covers the held re-stamp (the PoC) and a live session at `effectiveAt`.
- **D-M2-2, any change counts on-chain.** The guard does not try to tell a split from a dividend reinvestment.
  A dividend update during a hold therefore also stops that token's on-chain valuation until the next live
  print, typically the open. Off-hours the desk is reduce-only anyway, and exits never value tokens. Marks are
  refused for at most one interval and then held at the basis multiplier (D-M2-3).
- **D-M2-3, bounded refusal, then a held mark at the basis multiplier (controller ruling on review I-1,
  replacing "refuse, do not hold").** An unbounded refusal stalled M-1's 30-day bound, the desk flow gate and
  settlement for a token that never reprices. A held mark at the new multiplier would book the 10x P&L into the
  share prices and the drawdown kill. Valuing at the multiplier of the price's basis is economically right; 0
  is the fallback when it is unknown.
- **D-M2-4, in-band Chainlink residual accepted (review M-3).** A Chainlink round seen before an in-band change
  (a dividend reinvestment, at most `multiplierBandBps`, 5% by default) is still published, divided by its own
  multiplier, under a live `publishedAt` after the change. It is a valid pre-change share price, but it predates
  the ex-dividend move, and the registry values it at the new multiplier. The overvaluation is at most the step
  (about the dividend yield) until the feed's next round. Dropping it too would leave the ticker without a price
  in session until that round (Chainlink is the only signing source on mainnet; deviation 0.5% / heartbeat 24 h),
  which stops trades and liquidations; the dividend-sized error was judged smaller.

**Residual.**

- After a pre-approved split, the issuer staging a further change hides the split's time. The guard then stays
  closed until the governance re-anchor plus a live print (I-2 above), so this costs liveness, not value.
  What remains is an in-band step hidden the same way: an in-band change followed by a staged one inside one
  hold window. That is a dividend-sized error, like D-M2-4.
- Per-share sources (Pyth: confirm-only on mainnet; median policy on testnet) rely on the token's
  `effectiveAt()` matching the exchange's split time.
- Basis multiplier accuracy: the on-chain anchor fallback for a live token-side change is within the band of
  the true pre-change multiplier (the anchor follows the token up to the band). Valuations in held marks can
  therefore be off by up to the anchor's drift; the events refine nothing once the on-chain value is known.
  Carry-forward covers the unknown case. A 0 needs a stored-mode token with two governance changes after the
  basis and no previous mark value.
- Mark scheduler catch-up after a refusal: the scheduler always marks the current period, and an exhausted old
  job gives way to the next one, so `MarkRegistry`'s `maxMarkAge` cannot wedge it (re-review 1).
- The I-2 state (a split whose time a staged change hides) also keeps every voluntary engine trade on that
  market reverting (`SplitWindow`), closes and margin withdrawals included, until the governance re-anchor (a
  timelock action on mainnet) plus a live print. Liquidations and `forceClose` still run.
- Existing BookrunnerDesk clones keep the previous RISK flatten (unbounded in the window): the fix needs the new
  desk implementation, i.e. new books. Before the live rehearsal: confirm no live clones exist, or recreate them.
- Stored mode (devnet / testnet) has no token-staged change, so an engine market only gets a close-only period
  if governance pre-approves the change with `setNextMultiplierAnchor` before the `setMultiplier`, and that
  transaction is public (testnet only).

## N-1: PoolEngine per-share positions across a split (new, from the M-2 review)

**Finding.** Severity **High if the engine is live**: RHX5 / PoolEngine is small caps or off at launch (owner
decision). Engine positions are sized in units of the market's price id (shares) and are never rebased, while a
split's `newUIMultiplier()` / `effectiveAt()` is public on-chain days ahead. A trader opens a short in session
before a 10:1 split; at the reopen the per-share price prints at 1/10, and the short books about 90% of its
notional (times leverage) from the pool, liquidating the other side. A reverse split gives the same to a long
(+900%). In an open session at `effectiveAt` the last pre-split print also stays tradable for up to the trade
price-age bound. Exploit test without the gate: `test_N1_exploit_withoutGate_shortTakes90PercentAtTheReopen`
(a 100-unit short at $100 ends with margin + about $9,000).

**Fix.** `StockTokenRegistry.splitGate(priceId, basisAt)` covers the market's token, or every component for an
index price id (reverse indexes kept at registration):
- `pending`: an out-of-band change is pre-approved (`nextMultiplierAnchor` beyond the band of the anchor) or
  staged on the token (`newUIMultiplier()` beyond the band of the active multiplier, `effectiveAt()` ahead);
- `window`: a token's `multiplierChangedAt` is after `basisAt`.

`PoolEngine._trade` reverts `SplitWindow` for every voluntary trade in the window (closes too: the price is in
pre-change shares) and `SplitPending` for new risk while pending (close-only). `withdrawMargin` with a position
is new risk and is gated the same way. Liquidations and the wind-down `forceClose` are not gated. Close-only
lasts from the pre-approval or staging until governance clears it (`setMultiplier(new)` +
`setNextMultiplierAnchor(0)`, RUNBOOK).

Tests: `contracts/test/audit/ext2026/N1EngineSplit.t.sol` covers the PoC short reverting (also a reverse split
staged without pre-approval), close-only for an existing position, every trade reverting in the window until the
reprice and then trading normally after the cleanup, an index market gated by a component's split, and an
in-band change not gated. Four of the six fail on the previous engine (error stubs only); the other two are the
exploit demonstration and the in-band control.

**Residual.** Positions opened before the announcement still take the per-share step at the reopen: close-only
gives them the time before `effectiveAt` to exit, but nothing forces them out. Rebasing positions by the
multiplier ratio, or a settlement at the pre-split price at `effectiveAt`, is the complete fix. Open; required
before the engine runs with meaningful caps.

**Deployment.** Redeploy PoolEngine (non-upgradeable; markets and pools are recreated, which is acceptable at
launch). It reads the registry from config at call time; no registry configured means no gate.

## M-3: every engine quote straddles the oracle, and the pool has a loss budget per period

**Finding.** `MMMandate.checkQuote` bounded the width (`spread >= minQuoteWidthBps`) and the skew
(`|skew| <= maxSkewBps`) separately. The engine fills a buy at `oracle x (1 + spread/2 + skew)` and a sell at
`oracle x (1 - spread/2 + skew)`, so with `maxSkew > minWidth / 2` (all three devnet charters: 8/25, 12/30,
10/25) a quote can sit entirely on one side of the oracle: at width 10 and skew -25 the pool sells 20 bps below
the oracle, at skew +25 it buys 20 bps above it. A desk key flipping the skew around a colluding trader's buy and
sell moves value from the pool to the trader with no price move, no rate limit and no budget. The audit also noted
that no invariant checked that pool equity cannot fall at a constant price, which would have caught it.

PoC (RHX5 terms: width 10, skew 25, pool $100k, taker fee 6 bps), reproduced on the audited code by
`test_M3_poc_flipSkewRoundTrips_poolNotDrained`: 25 round trips of $70k in one block, 50 one-sided quotes
accepted, pool equity -$7,000.000000, trader margin +$4,900.000000 (the rest is taker fee). It repeats every
block until the drawdown kill at the next mark.

**Fix.**
1. *The quote straddles the oracle.* `MMMandate.checkQuote` requires `2 x |skew| <= spread` (`QuoteOneSided`),
   whatever the mandate's terms, so a mandate cached before the charter rule (the terms of an existing book are
   never re-validated) is covered too. `PoolEngine.setQuote` enforces the same bound itself (`InvalidQuote`), so
   no mandate, adapter or desk bug can put the engine quote on one side of the oracle. With it, a buy always
   fills at or above the oracle and a sell at or below it: at a constant oracle price no fill can cost the pool.
   `PoolEngineAdapter.applyMandate` keeps the bound by construction (it only widens the spread and narrows the
   skew).
2. *Terms.* `validateMandate` (re-mandates) and `MarketCharter.validate` (filing, approval) return `BAD_MANDATE`
   when `maxSkewBps > minQuoteWidthBps / 2` (integer: `2 x maxSkew > minWidth`). Mirrored in the charter service
   validator, the API's local validator and the web and site charter forms (default skew 4 at width 8). Devnet
   launch terms are now 8/4 (NVDA), 12/6 (TSLA) and 10/5 (RHX5).
3. *Per-period pool loss budget* (defence in depth for any loss channel the quote rule does not cover: price moves
   on inventory, pull-oracle latency (L-2), bad debt beyond the IF). Parameters and defaults:
   - period: `config.markInterval()`-long buckets of Unix time (`block.timestamp / markInterval + 1`; 1 hour if the
     interval reads 0), so the budget resets at most one mark interval after it is spent;
   - anchor: the pool equity marked at the oracle at the period's first trade (before the trade; `_poolEquity`,
     the same mark as `poolEquityUsd`); liquidity the adapter deposits or withdraws in the period shifts it, so a
     recall is not a loss and a deposit does not refill the budget;
   - budget: `lossBudgetBps` of the anchor. The adapter sets `|mandate.killAtDrawdownBps|` (the loss the mandate
     already treats as fatal for the book) at `initialize` and on every re-mandate (`applyMandate`), clamped to
     1..5000; a market created without the adapter starts at `DEFAULT_LOSS_BUDGET_BPS` = 800. No new charter field.
   - rule: a new-risk trade (open, increase, flip) reverts `LossBudgetExceeded(marketId, loss, budget)` while
     `anchor - poolEquity > budget` after the trade. Reductions, closes, liquidations and `forceClose` are never
     blocked (exits stay open). Every new-risk trade re-checks the cumulative loss of the period, so the number of
     trades or blocks does not matter: at most one budget (plus the positions already open, which can only close)
     per mark interval. `test_M3_lossBudget_capsManyTradesInOneBlock`: 25 latency round trips of $50k on a 2% swing
     in one block, 9 opens, 16 refused, pool loss $8,545 against an $8,000 budget.
   - cost: one SLOAD (the new `lossPeriod` / `lossAnchorUsd` slot) and one `config.markInterval()` read per trade,
     the loss check reuses the pool equity `_postTradeChecks` already computes; one SSTORE and a
     `LossPeriodStarted` event at the period's first trade. Measured with `PullOracleGasTest`: +3,891 gas per trade
     (193,339 -> 197,230 for the legacy trade, 226,113 -> 230,004 for a pull trade with a new price).
   - view: `PoolEngine.lossBudget(marketId)` returns the bps, period, anchor, loss, budget and whether new risk is
     blocked (agent, risk service, operators).
   - kill path: unchanged and as the mandate defines it. The book's on-chain drawdown kill at the next mark
     (`killAtDrawdownBps`) and the risk service's intra-mark DRAWDOWN breach; a RISK or committee kill when the
     loss is not market movement. The budget itself is never lifted by hand: the next period re-anchors.
4. *Off-chain mirror.* `packages/shared` `checkQuote` adds the `ONE_SIDED` violation (`bid <= oracle <= ask`,
   `quoteStraddlesOracle`) and `classifyLimits` counts it as a SKEW breach. The agent caps the skew at
   `min(maxSkew, h / (1 + h)) - safety` (h = half the width, relative to the mid, so the quote straddles the
   oracle exactly) for every book, and an engine SetQuote at `floor(spread / 2)`; `paramsWithinMandate` refuses
   anything else. A cached pre-fix mandate therefore never produces a quote the chain rejects. The risk monitor
   checks live quotes against the attested oracle price only (see Residuals), with a bounded drift allowance
   (`checkQuote(m, q, driftBps)`, `classifyLimits({ quoteDriftBps })`). "Fresh" here means at most
   `RISK_QUOTE_PRICE_MAX_AGE_MS` old (default 15 s), not the 300 s valuation bound. Without such a price no quote is
   checked and the book is held reduce-only whatever the agent quotes; the hold lifts after
   `RISK_QUOTE_HOLD_RELEASE_TICKS` (default 2) consecutive fresh ticks.

**Missing invariant.** `contracts/test/audit/ext2026/M3PoolEquityInvariant.t.sol` drives the real in-house stack
(MMMandate clone with the pre-fix 10/25 terms, BookrunnerDesk clone, PoolEngineAdapter proxy, PoolEngine,
AttestedOracle) at a constant oracle price: the colluding desk key quotes any spread / skew / cap through
`desk.execute`, the adapter sets raw engine quotes (only the engine's own bounds apply), the committee re-mandates
with arbitrary terms (and the key is re-registered), three colluding traders deposit, withdraw, open, flip, reduce,
close and liquidate, and time moves in steps of 1 to 12 s with the same price re-published. Invariants: pool equity
never falls below its starting value (allowance: 4 + 1 micro-USD per trade, every engine rounding is in the pool's
favour), the coalition never takes out (withdrawn + equity) more than it deposited, with every position closed the
realised pool cash never falls (zero tolerance), and conservation. `afterInvariant` requires every run that attempts
a trade to fill at least one (the handler tops up margin, re-quotes after a re-mandate and bounds sizes below the
smallest cap), so a fixture change that makes all trades revert cannot turn the suite into a no-op. Default
profile 256 x 128 calls; CI profile 800 x 128 = 102,400 calls, all passing. On the audited code the three economic
invariants and the campaign fail (the first version shrank to four calls: a re-mandate, a deposit, a one-sided
quote and a sell; conservation holds, the loss is pure transfer); the deterministic campaign repeats the auditor's
flip 25 times and then 600 random steps.

**Tests.** `contracts/test/audit/ext2026/M3PoolDrainSkew.t.sol` (shared fixture `utils/M3Stack.sol`): the PoC
(fails on the audited code with pool equity 93,000 < 100,000; now 50 quotes refused, the legs fill at the straddling
quote, pool +$1,750, trader -$3,850), `test_M3_poc_oneSidedQuote_refused` (also fails on the audited code),
exact errors (`QuoteOneSided`, `InvalidQuote`; a 50 bps quote may still use the full 25 bps skew), the engine bound
fuzzed (`setQuote` succeeds iff `2 x |skew| <= spread`, then buy fill >= oracle >= sell fill), the round trip at the
largest skew still allowed (pool never loses), `validateMandate` on the three devnet charters and their corrected
terms, `remandate(10/25)` reverting, and the budget tests (wiring from the mandate kill and bounds, blocks new risk
until the next period while reductions and closes run, many trades in one block capped, liquidity flows); the
PoC also asserts all 50 legs filled and no one-sided quote landed, so it cannot pass vacuously. Existing
fixtures moved to valid terms (8/4, 10/5, 15/3, 15/4); `MandateInvariant` checks the straddle on every quote that
reached the adapter; the engine fuzz tests bound the skew to half the spread.

**Residual.**
- The budget is a bound, not a detector: losses below it within a period, and the positions open when it bites
  (they can only close), are not stopped; the drawdown kill at the mark remains the backstop.
- Period buckets are aligned to Unix time, not to the book's actual marks: a loss straddling a bucket boundary can
  spend two budgets back to back, still bounded per interval.
- A spent budget blocks every trader's new risk for the rest of the period, including trades that would reduce
  the pool's exposure (the same semantics as reduce-only). It cannot be triggered without the pool really losing.
- The budget measures pool equity only; an IF draw (bad debt the IF covers) is not counted, an ADL (beyond the IF)
  is.
- Orderly books have no on-chain quote check: the rule is enforced by the agent clamp and the risk monitor (SKEW
  breach), as before for width and skew. The risk monitor checks every live quote against the **attested** price
  (fresh on-chain stored price or verified signed print; the unsigned Redis fallback does not count), never the
  reference the agent reports, with a print-drift allowance `RISK_QUOTE_DRIFT_BPS` (default 5, 0..10, capped at half
  the min quote width: 4 bps at 8 / 4) added to `maxSkewBps` and allowed past the oracle on each side. An agent can
  therefore hide at most that allowance of skew. The attested price must be at most `RISK_QUOTE_PRICE_MAX_AGE_MS`
  old (default 15 s; a held market-closed print counts: it is signed and current, and the book is reduce-only
  off-hours anyway). Without one (e.g. a missed signed-print read while the stored on-chain price is minutes old),
  the book is held `reduce_only` whatever is quoted, never killed on SKEW, until 2 consecutive fresh ticks.
  Re-review findings, both replaced: the intermediate `RISK_QUOTE_REF_TOLERANCE_BPS` (default 25) trusted an agent
  reference within 25 bps of the attested price; a first fail-safe hold depended on the agent's own quote (it
  flapped once the agent complied) and treated a price up to 300 s old as fresh.
- Agents that ignore the hold: a colluding agent can ignore `reduce_only`; during an attested-price outage only the
  NAV drawdown kill bounds it. The agent cannot cause that outage (prints are verified against on-chain signers).
- On a quiet chain risk holds once the chain head lags ~11 s while the agent stops only at 30 s, so books sit
  reduce-only in between (fail safe); the hold has no alert of its own yet (Task 7).
- At tight terms the allowance is small: a quote at the agent's maximum skew (`maxSkew - 0.5`) with print drift
  beyond the remaining room in the same direction is flagged; `RISK_BREACH_CONFIRM_TICKS` (2) filters a one-tick
  lag, a persistent one kills (fail safe).
- Zero-edge boundary: at `2 x |skew| == spread` one side fills exactly at the oracle. On a zero-taker-fee charter
  (devnet NVDA / TSLA) a colluding desk can hand a trader a spread-free side for pull-oracle latency arbitrage
  (L-2); the pool is never filled worse than the oracle and the budget bounds the loss. A minimum per-side edge
  (e.g. `2 x |skew| <= spread - 2`, or a charter field `minSideEdgeBps`) or a minimum engine taker fee is a
  possible future charter rule; the charter rule checks note the boundary (`mandate_quote` info).
- Anchor print: the first trader of a period chooses which recent signed print anchors it (pull oracle: any
  print newer than the stored one and within `maxPriceAge`); the extra headroom is bounded by |pool net
  exposure| x the spread between those prints.
- No breach event: a spent budget reverts the trade, so nothing is emitted; the agent and risk service do not read
  `lossBudget()` yet (alert / breach class: Task 7).

**Deployment.** Redeploy PoolEngine (non-upgradeable; markets and pools are recreated, which is acceptable at
launch and is the same redeploy N-1 needs), MarketCharter (non-upgradeable, as for M-1) and RiskCommittee
(non-upgradeable; its REMANDATE proposal check compiles the same `CharterRules.mandateReason`, so an old committee
still accepts 8/25 proposals), then `config.setAddresses(["poolEngine","charter","committee"], [new, new, new])`
and re-seat the members on the new committee; `BookFactory.setImplementations(["MANDATE", "ENGINE_ADAPTER"],
[newMandate, newAdapter])` through the timelock so new books get the new `checkQuote` and the budget wiring.
In-house books are **recreated on the new engine, never upgraded in place**: an existing adapter proxy (UUPS)
upgraded to the new implementation while still bound to the old engine would call `setLossBudget` from
`applyMandate`, which the old engine does not have, so every `remandate` of that book (and so the recovery of a
killed book) would revert. The new engine refuses a one-sided quote whatever the mandate, so the engine redeploy
alone closes the drain. Existing Orderly-book MMMandate clones keep the old `validateMandate`: only the new
committee refuses a re-mandate of the old terms (`BAD_MANDATE` at proposal); their cached 8/25, 12/30, 10/25 terms
are clamped by the agent and should be re-mandated to valid ones. Agent and risk service ship together (RUNBOOK),
with the charter service, API, web and site, from the same commit.

## M-4: receipts proofs and roots cached

**Finding.** `receipts.proof` and `receipts.root` (public, `/trpc/`) rebuilt an hour's Merkle tree on every
call. A 10-call batch cost 160-840 ms of CPU on Bun's single thread; one IP at the nginx limit (20 req/s) used
3.2-16.7 s of API CPU per second, stalling the API, the app and `/status` for everyone. No funds at risk.

**PoC, reproduced** (`services/api/bench/receipts-poc.ts 500`, one closed hour of 500 leaves, fake read model,
so DB time is excluded):

| | audited code | fixed |
|---|---|---|
| 10-call GET batch of `receipts.proof` | HTTP 200, 691 ms CPU | HTTP 400 before any procedure runs (~0 ms) |
| unbatched `receipts.proof`, first call of the hour | 62 ms | 63 ms (builds and caches the tree) |
| unbatched `receipts.proof`, later calls of the hour | 65.6 ms each | 1.1 ms each |
| one IP at the old limit (20 req/s of 10-call batches) | ~13.8 s of API CPU per second | refused (batch weight) |
| one IP at the receipts limit (2 r/s, one call per request), cached hours | — | ~2 ms of API CPU per second |

Tree building dominates (~150 us per leaf: the OpenZeppelin StandardMerkleTree, JS keccak and ABI encoding).

**Fix.**

- **Cache** (`services/api/src/domain/receiptCache.ts`). A closed hour (its `receipt_roots` row exists and the
  window has ended) is immutable, so its tree is built once and kept in an LRU bounded by entries (256) and
  total leaves (100,000, ~700 bytes each, so ~70 MB; `API_RECEIPT_CACHE_LEAVES`). An hour larger than the bound
  is not cached. The cached tree of a closed hour is the committed one and is never replaced by a tree that
  no longer matches the stored root: if receipts were written into the hour after its root (a producer clock
  fault), the committed tree is the first `leafCount` leaves by id (the receipts service rule), and the late
  receipts are proven against a separate tree of all the hour's leaves (`matchesStored: false`, which is
  true); every other proof keeps reporting the stored root, whatever order the requests come in. An hour whose
  leaves reproduce neither is logged and served consistently from the tree of all its leaves. An hour without a
  committed root is reused for 5 s only and never cached when it has no leaf (an arbitrary `hourStart` cannot
  evict real trees); expired entries are purged before any eviction. Concurrent misses on one hour share a
  single read and build. The tree carries a leaf index, so a proof no longer scans the hour. Period trees (a mark's
  `receiptsRoot` over its hourly roots) are cached per mark once they match it.
- **One source of truth.** The receipts service does not persist proofs (it builds them on demand), so the API
  still derives trees from the stored leaves; no second copy in the DB.
- **Batch weight** (`services/api/src/app.ts`): a `receipts.*` call weighs a full batch (`TRPC_HEAVY_WEIGHT =
  MAX_TRPC_BATCH`), so it must travel alone; the check runs before tRPC parses any input. The web client sends
  `receipts.*` through an unbatched `httpLink` (`apps/web/src/api/trpc.ts` `splitLink`).
- **`receipts.root` by hour checks the book exists** (`NOT_FOUND` before any receipt read).
- **nginx**: a stricter per-IP zone `bookrunner_receipts` (2 r/s, burst 10), keyed by a `map` on `$uri` that is
  empty for every `/trpc/` path without a `receipts.*` call, on top of `bookrunner_trpc` (20 r/s).

**Residual.** A cold miss still costs one build (~60 ms for 500 leaves, ~0.5 s for 3,000). An attacker cycling
through more distinct busy hours than the cache holds pays that per request, bounded by the receipts zone
(2 r/s per IP) and one build per hour in flight. An hour above the leaf bound is built on every call. The
nginx route stamp fails open: a future nginx location proxying to the API without `X-Bookrunner-Route` would
reach `/v1` and `/mcp` again (`deploy/server/README.md` rule; `apps/site/test/audit-nginx-paths.test.ts`
pins the proxied locations).

## L-3: nginx path confusion

**Finding.** `location ^~ /trpc/` used `proxy_pass http://127.0.0.1:4400;` (no URI), so the raw request path was
forwarded. Bun resolves `\` as `/` and dot-segments before the app sees the URL, so `/trpc/..\v1\books` reached
the REST mirror, the admin-token check of `/v1/webhooks` and `/mcp` (which accepted a 100-message batch). The
data is read-only and public.

**Fix.**

- `deploy/server/nginx-bookrunner-locations.conf`, `/trpc/`: a raw path (`$request_uri` before `?`) with a
  backslash, `%2F`, `%5C`, `%2E` or a dot-segment is a 400; the normalized `$uri` must be procedure names only
  (`^/trpc/[A-Za-z0-9._,-]+$`), else 400; the path is forwarded normalized (`proxy_pass
  http://127.0.0.1:4400/trpc/;`). `/trpc/`, `/health` and `/status.json` set `X-Bookrunner-Route` to their
  upstream path. The query string is not inspected (tRPC inputs are URL-encoded JSON).
- API (defence in depth, `services/api/src/app.ts` `pathAllowed`): a request carrying `X-Bookrunner-Route`
  must stay inside it (prefix for `/trpc/`, exact for `/health` and `/status`), and any path with an encoded
  slash, backslash or dot, or a dot-segment, is a 400 on every route. Loopback tooling without the header keeps
  `/v1` and `/mcp`.
- MCP: at most 10 JSON-RPC messages per POST (`MCP_MAX_BATCH`, the SDK allows 100) and a 64 KiB body.

**Verification.** `bash deploy/server/nginx-path-check.sh` runs `nginx -t` on the real http conf and locations
snippet in Docker (`nginx:1.27`) with a stand-in API that echoes what it receives, then sends raw requests over
`/dev/tcp` (no client-side normalisation). Fixed config: 0 failures (backslash, `%5C` / `%5c`, `/trpc/./`,
`/trpc/book.get/x` → 400; `%2F`, `%2e%2e`, `../` resolve outside `/trpc/` → static 404, never the API; normal
calls, `/health` and `/status.json` reach the API normalized and stamped; `receipts.*` gets 429 after the
burst, `book.get` does not). Audited config: 17 failures (`/trpc/..\v1\books` and the others forwarded raw).
`services/api/test/audit-path-confusion.test.ts` replays the PoC over a raw Bun socket: unstamped, Bun serves
`/v1/books` (why the nginx fix is primary); stamped `/trpc/`, the API answers 400.
